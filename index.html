Research

Field-level inference for galaxy clustering and CMB lensing

Galaxy surveys map the three-dimensional distribution of matter, and the standard analysis compresses that map into a power spectrum. The compression discards information: distinct fields can share the same power spectrum, and the non-Gaussian structure produced by non-linear gravitational evolution is lost.

Field-level inference avoids the compression. It infers the initial density field that evolved into the observed one, sampling a posterior over millions of parameters. This requires a forward model that can be differentiated end to end, together with a gradient-based sampler.

My thesis extends this framework to a second probe. Galaxies are biased tracers of the matter field, whereas the CMB lensing convergence is an unbiased projection of the total matter along the line of sight. A joint field-level analysis reduces degeneracies that neither probe constrains on its own, in particular on the amplitude of matter fluctuations and on local primordial non-Gaussianity.

The pipeline has been validated on synthetic data and on AbacusSummit N-body simulations. The next step is the analysis of DESI and Planck/ACT data.

The forward model proceeds as follows.

  1. Initial conditionsA Gaussian field, optionally with local primordial non-Gaussianity.
  2. Gravitational evolutionLagrangian perturbation theory on a light cone, in full spherical geometry.
  3. Galaxy bias and RSDBiased tracers placed in redshift space.
  4. Lensing projectionConvergence maps under the Born approximation, on the curved sky.
  5. Joint likelihoodPixel-exact, with the out-of-box lensing signal marginalised analytically.
  6. Posterior samplingMicrocanonical Langevin Monte Carlo on multiple GPUs.

Thesis pipeline · Python and JAX · MIT licence

thesis-desi-cmb-fli

Differentiable forward model and Bayesian inference pipeline for the joint field-level analysis of galaxy clustering and CMB lensing. Curved-sky light cone, LPT evolution, Born lensing projector, MCLMC sampling on multiple GPUs. Unit tests, continuous integration and documentation.
